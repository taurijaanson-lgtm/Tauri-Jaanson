01 — Business planning

Business plans and feasibility studies

A plan is a decision document. Ours are structured around the question the reader actually has to answer: can this company repay, deliver and survive a bad year?

  • Market sizing and competitor analysis backed by named sources
  • Commercial model: pricing, channels, unit economics, capacity
  • Operations, team, permits, suppliers and procurement plan
  • Investment budget split into eligible and non-eligible cost lines
  • Risk register with mitigations, sensitivities and a stress case
  • Executive summary that stands on its own for a credit committee

Typical length 25–45 pages plus annexes. Delivered in your template if you have one, or in the funder’s mandatory format.

02 — Financial modelling

Models built to be questioned

Every number traces back to a driver you can change. No hard-coded values hidden in formulas, no circular references, no tab nobody dares to touch. When a lender asks “what happens if volumes drop 20%?”, you answer in the meeting, not next week.

  • Integrated three-statement model with a balance sheet that balances
  • Monthly cash-flow for year one — the year that actually breaks companies
  • Scenario switch and one-way sensitivity tables on the drivers that matter
  • Covenant and debt-service coverage testing against your term sheet
  • Valuation support: DCF, comparables, cap table and dilution where relevant
  • Handover session so your finance lead owns the file afterwards
03 — Funding & grants

Applications, lenders and the paperwork after the “yes”

Funding is a process with rules. We screen eligibility before you spend money on the application, assemble the dossier to the letter of the guidelines, and stay on the file through clarification questions, contracting and reporting.

  • Eligibility screening and a realistic assessment of your odds — before you commit
  • Funding structure: own contribution, grant share, bank debt, leasing, working capital
  • Complete application dossier, annexes, budget justification and indicators
  • Bank and investor packs: teaser, plan, model, Q&A preparation
  • Responses to funder clarification requests within the deadline
  • Post-award: payment claims, progress reports, budget amendments, audit files

We work with national and EU-level instruments, commercial banks, leasing companies and private investors. We never promise a decision — we make sure the application cannot be rejected on quality.

04 — Project management

The project finishes the way it was approved

Most funding is lost after approval, not before: a cost line spent in the wrong category, a procurement run without the right documentation, a report filed late. We manage the administrative spine of the project so the technical team can build.

Engagements run monthly, either as full project management or as an oversight role alongside your own project manager — a second pair of eyes on budget and compliance.

Questions

Before you get in touch

Do you work with companies outside Estonia?

Yes. Work is done remotely across the EU, in English or Estonian. For grant applications we need the specific programme guidelines; if the scheme is one we have not handled before, we say so and price the extra research into the fee.

Will you sign an NDA?

Always, and usually before the first call if you prefer. Client names are never published without written permission — that is why the case studies on this site are anonymised.

Can you guarantee the funding is approved?

No, and be careful with anyone who does. The funder decides. What we control is quality: an application that meets every formal requirement, a model that survives scrutiny, and an honest assessment up front if we think the odds are poor.

What do you need from us to start?

Last two or three years of financial statements, current-year figures, the investment budget or supplier quotes, and one person who can answer questions within a day or two. We send a short checklist after the scoping call.

Do you take a success fee instead of a fixed fee?

Our default is a fixed fee, because it keeps the advice honest. For larger funding transactions a partial success component can be agreed, always on top of a reduced base fee and always written into the proposal.
